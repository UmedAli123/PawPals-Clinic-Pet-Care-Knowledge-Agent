PawPals Pet Clinic - Pet Dental Care

More than 80 percent of dogs and cats show signs of dental disease by age 3. Bad dental health can lead to pain, tooth loss and infections that spread to the heart and kidneys.

Daily brushing
Brush your pet's teeth daily if possible, or at least 3 times a week. Use a pet toothpaste only. Human toothpaste contains fluoride and xylitol, which can be harmful to pets. Start slowly by letting your pet lick the toothpaste, then introduce a soft brush.

Dental chews and diets
Dental chews and special diets can help reduce plaque. Choose products with the VOHC seal of acceptance, and avoid very hard items such as bones and antlers, because they can break teeth.

Professional cleaning
Most pets benefit from a professional dental cleaning once a year. The procedure is done under anaesthesia so the vet can clean below the gum line and take dental X-rays.

Warning signs
Bad breath, red or bleeding gums, drooling, dropping food, pawing at the mouth, or a reluctance to eat hard food are signs your pet needs a dental check.

Book a dental check
If you notice any of these signs, book an exam with the clinic. Early treatment is cheaper and safer than waiting.
