PawPals Pet Clinic - Cat Nutrition Guide

Cats are obligate carnivores. They need a diet high in animal protein and cannot get all their nutrients from plant food.

Kittens
Kittens up to 12 months old should eat food labelled for growth. Feed 3 to 4 small meals each day until 6 months of age, then reduce to 2 meals per day.

Adult cats
Most adult cats do well on 2 measured meals per day. Check the feeding chart on the food package and adjust to keep a healthy body weight. You should be able to feel your cat's ribs under a thin layer of fat.

Senior cats
Cats older than 10 years may need food with easy-to-digest protein and controlled phosphorus. Ask the clinic before switching to a senior or kidney support diet.

Wet and dry food
Wet food adds water to the diet and can help urinary health. Dry food is convenient and can be left out for a short time. A mix of both is common. Change foods slowly over 7 to 10 days by mixing the new food with the old food in growing amounts.

Water
Always provide fresh water. Many cats prefer a wide, shallow bowl or a water fountain.

Foods to avoid
Never feed onions, garlic, chocolate, grapes, raisins, alcohol or caffeine. Milk can cause stomach upset in many adult cats.
