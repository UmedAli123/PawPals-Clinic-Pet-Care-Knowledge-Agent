PawPals Pet Clinic - When to Seek Emergency Care

Some symptoms need urgent veterinary care. If you are unsure, call the clinic first. If the clinic is closed, go to the nearest emergency animal hospital.

Go immediately if your pet has:
Difficulty breathing or constant coughing, collapse or inability to stand, seizures lasting more than 3 minutes, heavy bleeding that does not stop after 5 minutes of pressure, a swollen hard belly with unproductive retching, suspected poisoning, or a straining male cat that cannot pass urine.

Common poisons
Chocolate, xylitol sweetener, grapes, raisins, onions, human medicines, rat poison and antifreeze are dangerous to pets. Do not try to make your pet vomit unless a vet tells you to.

Call the same day if your pet has:
Vomiting or diarrhoea lasting more than 24 hours, refusal to eat for more than a day, limping that does not improve, eye injuries, or a wound that looks infected.

What to tell us
Share your pet's species, age, weight, the symptoms, when they started and anything your pet may have eaten. If possible, bring the packaging of any suspected poison.

Be prepared
Keep the clinic number and the emergency hospital address saved in your phone.
